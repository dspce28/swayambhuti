Most SME owners I meet don't have a growth problem. They have a **memory problem**. The business runs on what the owner remembers, and every new customer, branch or hire adds load to one person's head.

Scaling a business like that doesn't multiply profit. It multiplies firefighting.

The fix is not a 200-page operations manual nobody reads. It is five short documents — each one page, two at most — covering the processes where a mistake costs you the most.

## 1. How a lead becomes a customer

Write down every step from first enquiry to signed order: who responds, within how many hours, what they send, when they follow up, and when a lead is marked lost.

If two salespeople handle the same enquiry differently, you don't have a sales process. You have two salespeople.

## 2. How an order is delivered

From confirmed order to delivered product or service. Include the hand-offs — sales to operations, operations to dispatch, dispatch to accounts — because that is where things fall through.

> Every hand-off without a written rule is a place where the owner gets a phone call.

## 3. How money comes in

Invoicing, payment terms, reminders and escalation. Who sends the invoice, on which day, and what happens on day 15, day 30 and day 45 if it isn't paid.

Cash flow problems in SMEs are very often follow-up problems in disguise.

## 4. How a new person starts

What a new hire does on day one, in week one and by day 30. Who trains them on what, and how you know they are ready.

Without this, every hire takes weeks of the owner's time — which is exactly the time you hired them to free up.

## 5. How a complaint is handled

Who owns a complaint, how fast the customer hears back, what the team is allowed to offer without asking, and when it reaches the owner.

Customers forgive mistakes. They don't forgive silence.

## How to write them so they get used

- **One page per process.** If it is longer, it is two processes.
- **Steps, not paragraphs.** Numbered, with an owner and a time limit on each.
- **Write it with the person who does the work.** They know the real process; you know the intended one.
- **Review every quarter.** An SOP that is out of date is worse than none, because people stop trusting all of them.

## Where to start this week

Pick the one process that generated the most phone calls to you last month. Sit with the person who runs it for 45 minutes and write it down together. That single page will save you more hours than any new software.
